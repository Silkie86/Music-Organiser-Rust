# The beta checklist on Settings > Developer (mo_app::beta). Each step is
#   name | what to do | what you should see
# under an "== Area" heading. A step's name is how its mark is kept in beta-checklist.json: never change or reuse one
# (reword the step instead; a step that's gone keeps its old mark in the file). A thread that builds something that
# needs trying in real use adds a step here. The Self-check steps (auto.*) are marked by the app itself, from what
# mo_app::self_check finds. Live tests with a DAW only use a project called
# "MO Live Test..." (rule 10).

== Start
start.release | Start the app from the launcher's Release shortcut, close it, then press Enter in the launcher window to start it again | It opens in a few seconds both times; Settings > Developer says Build: Release | 1) Close DAWmate if it's open. 2) Double-click the launcher's Release shortcut and wait for the window. 3) Close it with the X. 4) In the launcher's black window press Enter. 5) When it's open, go to Settings > Developer and read the This build card
start.maximised | Start the app from the launcher | It opens filling the screen, with its title bar and the taskbar showing (maximised), on your widest screen too | Start DAWmate the way you normally do and look at the window before touching anything: the title bar should be at the very top and the taskbar visible below, and it should reach both edges of your ultrawide
start.full-screen | Press F11, minimise the app and bring it back, close it and start it again; then F11 again | Full screen stays after a minimise and the next start opens full screen; the second F11 leaves the window filling the screen (maximised), and the start after that opens maximised | 1) Press F11: the window fills the whole screen. 2) Click the minimise button (or Win+D), then click DAWmate on the taskbar: it should still be full screen. 3) Close the app and start it again: it should open full screen. 4) Press F11 once more: it should go back to filling the screen with the taskbar showing. 5) Close and start again: it opens maximised
start.close-clean | Use the app for a while, then close it (Alt+F4, or F11 out of full screen and the window's X) | It closes at once; no new crash-....log in the logs folder | 1) Use a few pages for five minutes or so. 2) Close with Alt+F4 (or press F11 to leave full screen and click the X). 3) The window should vanish straight away. 4) Open the logs folder (Settings > Developer, This build, click the logs line) and check no new file starting crash- appeared

== Install
install.setup | From the Releases page on GitHub, download the DAWmate setup and run it (Windows may ask: More info, then Run anyway). Keep your data folder where it is | No administrator question; DAWmate is in the Start menu and opens with your songs and sounds as before | 1) Go to the app's Releases page on GitHub and download the newest DAWmate-setup .exe. 2) Run it; if Windows shows a blue box, click More info, then Run anyway. 3) Keep the data folder it suggests (your I: folder). 4) Finish, then open DAWmate from the Start menu
install.update | Once a newer test version is out, leave the installed DAWmate open a day (or start it the next day). The launcher's window can stay open: its new builds wait while the installed DAWmate is open | The rail says a new version is ready; Restart to update closes the app and opens the new version, with everything kept | Use the installed DAWmate (Start menu), not the launcher's copy. When Claude says a newer test version is out, leave it open a day, or start it the next day. A line on the left rail says an update is ready: press Restart to update. The first update (from 0.9.0-beta.1) is started by beta.1's own code: if DAWmate opens as the old version again, press Restart to update once more and tell Claude, who reads the setup's log (the newest Setup Log file in your Temp folder)

== Songs
songs.list | Open the Songs page, search for a song, pick a few chips | The list fills at once and the search and chips narrow it | 1) Click Songs on the left. 2) Type part of a song's name in the search box at the top. 3) Click a few of the chips under it (a DAW, a year, a key) and watch the list get shorter. 4) Clear the search and chips again
songs.open-each-daw | Open one FL song, one Bitwig song and one Studio One song from the Songs card | Each opens in its own DAW | 1) On Songs, click an FL Studio song, then the Open in... button on its card. 2) Do the same for a Bitwig song and a Studio One song. Each should open in the right program
songs.preview | Pick ten songs of different ages, from all three DAWs | Each song's arrangement preview shows its clips, with bars of the right length | Click songs one after another: some old, some new, some from each DAW. Under each song's card there's a picture of its arrangement: the coloured clips should sit in the right places and the bar lines should look right
songs.preview-old | Pick a Bitwig song made before Bitwig 6, and a Studio One song with takes or layers | The preview isn't empty | 1) Find a Bitwig song from before Bitwig 6 (sort or filter by year). 2) Find a Studio One song where you recorded takes or layers. 3) Click each and look at the arrangement picture
songs.time-signature | Pick a song in 3/4 or 6/8 if you have one | The bars in the preview are the right length | Search for a song you know is in 3/4 or 6/8 (a waltz feel, three beats to a bar). Click it and check the bar lines in the picture match its bars. Skip this step if you have none
songs.fix | On Space's Bitwig copies card, tick one song and use Fix, then open its "(fixed)" project in Bitwig (Songs has no Fix of its own yet) | The original is untouched; the fixed project opens and plays with nothing missing | 1) Go to Space, find the Bitwig copies card. 2) Tick one song and press Fix. 3) Open the new project with (fixed) in its name in Bitwig. 4) Play it and look for any missing sample warning. 5) Check the original project is still there, unchanged
songs.to-bitwig | On an FL song, use Open in Bitwig | A Bitwig project opens with the same tracks, clips and tempo | 1) On Songs, click an FL Studio song. 2) Press Open in Bitwig on its card. 3) When Bitwig opens it, compare its tracks, clips and tempo with the same song in FL
songs.to-bitwig-automation | In that converted song, look at tempo, volume, pan and on/off automation | It moves the same way as in FL | In the Bitwig project from the step before, open the automation lanes (the arrow by each track) and play: tempo changes, volume and pan moves and mutes should happen at the same places as in FL
songs.todos | On a song's card, add three to-dos, tick one, take one off with its x, then close and start the app again | The list keeps them through the restart, the song's line shows how many are left, and Has to-dos under SHOW ONLY shows only songs with some left | 1) On Songs, click a song. 2) In its TO-DO box type something and press Enter, three times. 3) Tick the first one, hover the second and click its x. 4) Close DAWmate and start it again: the song should still have them, with a small list sign and a 1 on its line. 5) Tick Has to-dos on the left: only songs with to-dos left stay
songs.next-up | Click Next up at the top of the Songs list, then tick a to-do there | Every to-do left shows under its song; the ticked one goes from Next up and shows ticked on that song's card | 1) Put to-dos on two or three songs. 2) Click Next up above the list. 3) Tick one of them: it leaves the list. 4) Click that song's name: its card opens with the to-do ticked
songs.time-spent | On Songs, look at the TIME column, then click its heading | FL songs show how long FL Studio has had them open (41h), and the list goes most time first | 1) Click Songs and wait a few seconds after the list shows. 2) FL songs get a time in the TIME column (Bitwig and others stay blank). 3) Click TIME: the songs you spent most time on come first. 4) Click a song: beside its saves line a clock shows the same time; rest the mouse on it to see it in full
songs.dust-off | Look at the DUST OFF card above the Songs list, then click the moon on one song | Up to three FL songs you spent an hour or more on and haven't saved for six months, not finished; the moon takes one off the card, and it stays off after a restart | 1) Click Songs: DUST OFF shows above the list headings (it hides while you search or filter). 2) Click a song on it: its card opens. 3) Click the moon at the right of one: it leaves the card. 4) Close DAWmate and start it again: it's still off. 5) Click the DUST OFF heading: the whole list puts those songs first
songs.bounces | On Songs, find a song you exported a mixdown of, open its card and play the bounce, then go to another page | Songs with an exported sound file beside their project show a speaker; the card's SOUND box lists them newest first; the bar at the bottom plays on across pages and pauses, jumps and closes | 1) Click Songs and wait a few seconds: songs you exported (a .wav or .mp3 named like the song, beside the project or in an Exports or Renders folder there) get a small speaker by the name. 2) Tick Has sound on the left: only those stay; No sound yet shows the rest. 3) Click one: its card has a SOUND box. 4) Click the play button on a line: the bar at the bottom plays it. 5) Click Sounds in the rail: it keeps playing. 6) Click on the bar's line to jump, the pause button to pause, the x to put the bar away

== Sounds

sounds.hide-duplicates | Switch Hide duplicates on and off | Only real copies of a sound hide; nothing else goes | On Sounds, switch Hide duplicates on: copies of the same sound in different folders hide, leaving one. Switch it off: they come back. Check nothing that isn't a copy went
sounds.like-favourites | Tick Favourites under SHOW ONLY, then move its Sounds like them slider | Similar sounds show within a second or two, and they sound alike | 1) On Sounds, under SHOW ONLY tick Favourites. 2) A Sounds like them slider appears: drag it to the right. 3) Within a second or two, similar sounds join the list: play a few to check they sound alike
sounds.bitwig-favourites | Open the Favourites from Bitwig card, tick your multisamples and press Mark as favourites | Most of your Bitwig favourites are found; Save as sounds puts them in "From Bitwig" | 1) On Sounds, open Favourites from Bitwig... 2) Tick the multisamples you want. 3) Press Mark as favourites. 4) Check most of your Bitwig favourites are found, then press Save as sounds and look in From Bitwig
sounds.drag-bitwig | Drag a sound from the app onto a Bitwig track | The sound lands on the track and plays | With Bitwig open beside the app, hold the mouse on a sound in DAWmate's list and drag it onto an audio track in Bitwig, then let go. Press play in Bitwig
sounds.drag-fl | Drag a sound from the app into FL's playlist or a channel | The sound lands and plays | With FL open beside the app, drag a sound from DAWmate's list into FL's playlist or onto a channel, let go, and play it
sounds.trim-silence | With an export folder on another drive set in Settings, drag a sound with Trim silence on | A trimmed copy is made in that export folder (not on C:), and that's what lands in the DAW | 1) In Settings > Folders set Exports to a folder on I: or another non-C: drive. 2) On Sounds, turn Trim silence on. 3) Drag a sound with silence at its start into your DAW. 4) Look in that export folder for the trimmed copy
sounds.send-bitwig | Send a sound, and a kit onto Drum Machine pads, to Bitwig | They arrive on the right track and pads | 1) In Bitwig, select a track. 2) In DAWmate pick a sound and use Send to Bitwig. 3) Then pick a kit and send it onto a Drum Machine: each sound should land on its own pad
sounds.galaxy | Open the Galaxy: zoom, pan, use the arrow keys, sweep over stars | 60 frames a second, sounds play as you sweep, grey dust for filtered-out stars isn't too faint | 1) On Sounds, open the Galaxy view. 2) Zoom with the wheel, drag to move around, use the arrow keys. 3) Move the mouse across stars: each one plays as you pass. 4) Filter something out and check its stars show as grey dust you can still see

== MIDI
midi.page | Open the MIDI page and pick a few MIDI files | It opens without a stall; the piano roll draws at once | Click MIDI on the left and click a few MIDI files. The page should open without freezing and the notes should draw at once
midi.play | Play a part, change the speed with the BPM stepper and switch the Metronome on | It plays in time and the changes are heard straight away | 1) On MIDI, pick a part and press Play. 2) Change the BPM with the + and - buttons while it plays. 3) Switch Metronome on: a click on each beat. Changes should be heard straight away
midi.play-fl | Play a part into FL Studio over the MIDI port | The selected FL channel plays it | 1) In FL, select a channel with an instrument. 2) In DAWmate's MIDI page, send the part to FL's MIDI port and play it: FL's channel should play the notes
midi.fits | On one of your songs, open Fits my song (for the two Bitwig steps below, pick your MO Live Test Bitwig project here, saved with its parts in the arranger) | A list of parts that fit comes up within a few seconds | 1) On MIDI, press Fits my song and pick one of your songs from the menu. 2) Wait a few seconds for the list of parts that fit. For the two Bitwig steps below, pick your MO Live Test Bitwig project here instead (saved in Bitwig with its parts in the arranger). The menu offers the 8 songs saved last, MO Live Test among them though the Songs page doesn't list it: if it isn't there, save it in Bitwig again first
midi.fits-play | Play with the song in the app, and try Octave up and down | It sounds in key and in time with the song | With a song picked in Fits my song, pick a part and press Play with the song. Then try Octave up and Octave down while it plays
midi.fits-by-hand | Pick the song's parts and bars by hand, close and reopen the song | Your pick is remembered | 1) In Fits my song, choose which of the song's parts and bars to fit to, by hand. 2) Close the song (pick another), then pick it again: your choice should still be there
midi.fits-bitwig | With Fits my song fitted to your MO Live Test project, open that project in Bitwig, select an instrument track, then use Play in Bitwig | Bitwig loops the song's bars with the part playing | 1) Pick your MO Live Test project in Fits my song (save it in Bitwig first if the menu doesn't offer it). 2) Open that same project in Bitwig. 3) Click an instrument track in Bitwig. 4) Pick a part in DAWmate and press Play in Bitwig
midi.fits-put-in-song | The same way (fitted to your MO Live Test project, open in Bitwig, an instrument track selected), use Put in the song, then Undo in Bitwig | The part is recorded at the right bars; Undo takes it out | As the step before (MO Live Test picked and open in Bitwig, an instrument track selected), press Put in the song. Leave Bitwig alone until it's done, check the part is at the right bars, then press Ctrl+Z in Bitwig (it can take a few presses)
midi.bar-line | Drag a MIDI part into Bitwig and FL and loop it | It loops cleanly with no extra bar at the end | Drag a MIDI part from DAWmate into Bitwig, loop it and listen; then do the same in FL. There should be no empty bar at the end of the loop

backup.gone | Delete a test file from your music, back up again | The backup keeps it, with a warning | 1) Make a test file in a backed-up folder and back up. 2) Delete the test file. 3) Back up again: the backup should keep it and warn that it's gone from your music

auto.errors | The app looks for error lines in its logs | None
auto.start | The app reads how long each start took to the window | Usually 5 s or less
